## 1. What the manuscript is

The manuscript is the mature form of a research program that reverses Heisenberg's 1925 move: it readmits trajectories and demotes the operator algebra to bookkeeping of a substrate. The substrate is modelled at its continuum level by a chiral Cosserat (micropolar) medium with an $SU(2)$-lifted orientation field, a Skyrme quartic term, a BPS-Skyrme sextic-plus-potential sector, and a parity-odd transduction sector. From this it derives, or claims to derive: Maxwell's equations as the locked transverse doublet; a longitudinal "second speed" $c_L$; mass as a band gap; charge as disclination winding; the electron as a degree-one texture with a Compton-scale evanescent halo; three families as frustration classes against a chiral condensate of pitch 26 μm; the neutrino as a unit-Hopf excitation of that condensate; the electroweak skeleton with $\rho=1$; dark energy as the lag of the pitch; and a single handedness bit linking weak chirality, $\delta_{CP}$ and cosmic birefringence.

Its best feature is structural: every claim carries a grade, an audit for circularity, and a kill condition. The program is arranged, in its own words, "to be efficiently wrong". That deserves to be taken seriously, and the right way to take it seriously is to check whether the kills are real, whether the grades are honest, and whether the structure hides predictions it has not drawn.

The website built from the earlier draft used a third form, $(\hbar/Mc)\,\kappa/\sqrt{1-\kappa^2} = 3.86\times10^{-13}$ m. These differ by a factor 2. Eq. 2. **The Weinberg-angle tilt (7.7).** Eq. If the window is the $\tau$'s, the electron is not near-BPS ($\epsilon_e \ge 0.08$); if it is the electron's (as Appendix H's $\epsilon_e \lesssim 8\times10^{-6}$ suggests), then $\epsilon_\tau \lesssim 10^{-10}$ and every place the window is used for a non-electron knot (Proposition 27's $N_\nu$, Proposition 24's EDM, Proposition 17's $\Delta n$) must be re-evaluated species by species.

### 2.3 Proofs that do not hold as printed

**Proposition 18 (Closure K-10).** Eq. (57) integrates $r^2(\pi p/r^2)^2$ and calls the result an energy. The stiffness $a_\psi$ has units energy/length (from $J\omega^2 = 2a_\psi k^2 + \tilde m^2$), so $4\pi^3 a_\psi p^2/r_b$ has units energy/length², and the gradient $(\pi p/r^2)$ is not the $\partial_r\psi\sim\pi p/r_b$ the proof describes. With a twist of fixed angle $\pi p$ spread over a shell of radius $r_b$, the gradient energy is $\sim 4\pi^3 a_\psi p^2 r_b$ and the potential energy $\sim(2\pi/3)\tilde m^2\pi^2p^2 r_b^3$; both *increase* with $r_b$, so the functional is minimised at the smallest $r_b$, the core, not at the halo. Eq. (59), "balancing" (57) and (58), yields $r_b^4 = 6a_\psi/\tilde m^2$, a length squared. The conclusion $r_b^*\sim\lambda_{\rm halo}$ is then asserted by "matching onto the screened halo mode". The physical claim (the belts live in the halo because the core is BPS-rigid and the gapped field cannot penetrate it) may well be right, and Section 4.9 argues that existing data *require* something like it, but it is not proven here, and K-10 feeds Stake S1 directly. The grade should return to [posed].

**Proposition 16 (the cliff width).** From Eq. (25), the smallest covering radius consistent with $N_{\rm dof}$ at $n$ qubits is $\delta_{\min} = c_0(c_0/3GD)^{1/(\alpha-1)}$, which does reproduce Eq. (52). But the 90%→10% window implied by that formula is not $\log_2\ln(3GD/c_0)$. Solving Eq. (25) at $\delta = \arccos\sqrt{0.9}$ and $\delta = \arccos\sqrt{0.1}$ gives a width of $\log_2[\ln(c_0/\delta_{90})/\ln(c_0/\delta_{10})] = 2.8$ qubits at $c_0 = \pi/2$, independent of $M = \ln(3GD/c_0)$ for $M\gg1$ (4.4). The paper's 6 is the span from the first qubit above $n_0$ (where $F\approx1$) to the 10% point. The correction makes the prediction sharper, not weaker: a dimension-counting cliff would be a wall three qubits wide.

**Theorem 7 as a test.** Theorem 7 bounds a substrate that represents *every* $n$-qubit state to within $\delta$. A mirror circuit prepares one state. The revision's "reachable-set theorem" makes the point that no polynomial-size circuit family can test a bound on the whole of $\mathbb{CP}^{2^n-1}$; what a circuit tests is whether the substrate stores the reachable states densely. The master manuscript's Proposition 16 and protocol are therefore tests of a *hypothesis* (dense storage), and the headline should say so. The hypothesis is still a good one, and the cliff is still gate-independent, which is what makes it killable.

## 3. Critical assessment, sector by sector

### 3.1 The substrate idea on its own terms

Strip away the Standard-Model vocabulary and GUM is a Bell-beable program with four ontological commitments: a primitive ontology in three-space (textures of $(\mathbf u, \tilde Q)$); the wave function as a conditional-field tower (Norsen's theory of exclusively local beables, truncated); a preferred frame; and finite-speed nonlocality carried by a longitudinal sector. The first two are mainstream-adjacent and well motivated (they answer Bell's question of what the "local beables" are). The last two are the price, and the manuscript pays it openly (Sec. 4.5: the cone-locking bill "is a real mechanism, but a logarithm buys only a factor of $10$–$10^2$ whereas cone universality is required at $10^{-15}$–$10^{-20}$").

What is distinctive, and what the mainstream reflex would miss, is that GUM converts interpretational commitments into *capacities*: a substrate with $N_{\rm dof}$ degrees of freedom has a finite Schmidt capacity, a finite representational dimension, a finite update speed. Each of these is a number, and each number is a kill. That is the right shape for a beables theory to take, and it is why the quantum-computing cliff (Section 3.4) is the manuscript's best idea even after the corrections of Section 2.3.

The weakest ontological joint is the one the manuscript calls its foundation. Proposition 23 (K-0) "derives" the Cosserat continuum from a causal-adjacency complex by assuming a statistically homogeneous, isotropic complex of spectral dimension 3 with $SU(2)$ edge comparators and a fixed-sign chiral triple-edge term. Those assumptions *are* the continuum postulates in combinatorial dress: isotropy and $d_s = 3$ give the flat reference metric; the $SU(2)$ comparator gives $\tilde Q$; the chiral triple term gives $W_\chi$; the gradient expansion is the standard Wilsonian one. Nothing is wrong with this, but it is an embedding theorem, not an emergence theorem: it shows that *if* the level-0 object already carries the structures, the continuum inherits them. The one genuinely non-trivial output, Objectivity F10′ as a consequence of background independence, is correct and worth keeping. The rest should be graded [DW-structural] honestly as "consistent with", not "derived from".

### 3.2 Electrodynamics: the orientation sector

Theorems 2–4 are correct and nicely put: MacCullagh's curl-only energy fails linearized objectivity as a Cauchy continuum (because $\partial_{[i}u_{j]}$ shifts under rigid rotation) and is rescued verbatim as $\tfrac14\gamma|\nabla\times\boldsymbol\varphi|^2$ in the micropolar theory, where the wryness $\Gamma_{ij}$ is objective. Maxwell's source-free equations then follow with $c^2 = \gamma/2J$ (checked, Appendix B). The gauge transformation as a re-convention of the orientation field is elegant. None of this is discriminating, as the audit table admits, but it is the kind of clean theorem a substrate program should be built on.

The departure, two-speed electrodynamics (Definition 1, Theorem 5), is where the manuscript and its own revision disagree. The manuscript says the longitudinal field of a charge updates at distance $r$ after $r/c_L$ and that an oscillating source radiates longitudinal energy at relative power $\eta(c/c_L)^3$. The revision argues that a longitudinal sector completed by transverse projection is acausal at every finite speed, and that the only local completion recovering Gauss's law is Maxwell's theory in the velocity gauge, with the "leak" turned into an energy bookkeeping artefact of relative size $\tfrac12(c/c_L)^3$; it withdraws S15. Reading both: the revision is right that a physical longitudinal update at $c_L > c$ would be a *precursor*, an acausal field in the laboratory frame, and that this is a much stronger claim than a suppressed radiation channel. The manuscript does not seem to have noticed that Theorem 5(ii) says this. Section 4.1 turns it into an experiment anyway, because a precursor is exactly the kind of thing that is cheap to look for and would be decisive.

The pitch sector (Sec. 3.5) is coherent. Proposition 2's transparency bound and Proposition 17's locking suppression are both reproduced; the margin is 54 decades. Two remarks. First, Proposition 17's suppression $(qa)^4$ uses $a^2 = \gamma/4\mu_c$, which makes "the structural scale" a *ratio of moduli*, not a lattice spacing; the manuscript elsewhere treats $a$ as the graph step. If these are different lengths the whole $a$-range bookkeeping needs two symbols. Second, with the suppression this strong the 26-μm feature is unobservable in the doublet by construction, so Stake S22 as printed ("non-redshifting 26 μm feature") is not a stake of GUM's: GUM predicts the feature is *absent*, and a detection would embarrass Proposition 17 rather than confirm the substrate. The revision's redshift-swept trough is the better observable.

### 3.3 Finite $c_L$ and the Bancal–Barnea dichotomy

This is the manuscript's most exposed commitment, and it is under-defended. The tower is "hosted by the B1 constraint sector at speed $c_L$" (Sec. 4.2), so the conditional fields update at finite speed in a preferred frame. Bell-test timing gives $c_L \gtrsim 10^4c$ (Salart et al.), which the manuscript cites. But Bancal, Pironio, Acín, Liang, Scarani and Gisin (2012) proved that any model in which hidden influences propagate at a finite speed $v>c$, *and which reproduces quantum correlations whenever the influence arrives in time*, permits superluminal signalling at the level of operational probabilities in a four-party configuration; Barnea et al. (2013) reduced it to three parties. The dichotomy is: GUM either deviates from quantum mechanics in such configurations (then it must say by how much, and that becomes the stake), or it signals (then it is not merely "non-local" but operationally superluminal in the substrate frame). The master manuscript has no theorem, no stake and no citation on this; the revision's Theorem 13 does. Section 4.2 proposes the experiment.

A related gap: Proposition 26 (K-9, multi-time Lüders consistency) is the Bohmian effective-collapse argument transplanted into a Nelson diffusion, with the empty branches switched off once pointer packets separate by more than $\lambda_{\rm halo}$. That is fine as far as it goes, but it is precisely the regime in which Nelson's own 2012 review (the manuscript's ref. 33) located the trouble with stochastic mechanics (two-time correlations), and the error terms in Eq. (70) include $r_{SM}/(c_L\Delta t)$, which is not small for fast sequential measurements at large separation. That makes "sequential measurements with $\Delta t < r/c_L$" a GUM-specific deviation, an experiment the manuscript does not list (Section 4.2 again).

### 3.4 The quantum bookkeeping and the cliff

Lemma 3 (depth is rank), Theorem 6 (Eckart–Young fidelity) and Corollary 1 (Marchenko–Pastur flat spectra) are correct. The capacity floor from random-circuit sampling, $\chi_{\max}\gtrsim1.3\times10^7$, is a legitimate and under-appreciated inference: a classical substrate that could only represent low-Schmidt-rank states across the middle cut would have shown up as a depth-independent deficit in cross-entropy benchmarking at 53–60 qubits. It did not.

Theorem 7 is a correct covering-number argument, and Corollary 2's numbers reproduce. The problems are the ones in Section 2.3: the theorem is about representing every state; the printed cliff width is wrong (it is ≈3 qubits); and $c_0$ must exceed 1.25 for the fidelity to reach 10%. One more remark the manuscript should make: $N_{\rm dof} = V/a^3$ counts cells, but each cell carries at least twelve real phase-space numbers, which shifts the ceiling by $\log_2 12 \approx 3.6$ qubits; this is inside the $O(\log_2\log)$ slop, but a prediction claimed to a few qubits should say what it counts.

Then there is the question the manuscript does not ask: *what fixes $a$?* Two of its own closures do. Eq. (75), the Sakharov-induced Planck mass $M_{\rm Pl}^2 = N_{\rm eff}\hbar c/(12\pi a^2)$, inverted with the measured $G$, gives $a = \sqrt{N_{\rm eff}/12\pi}\,\ell_{\rm P}$: 0.40 $\ell_{\rm P}$ for $N_{\rm eff} = 6$, 1.26 $\ell_{\rm P}$ for 60 (8.6). With $a\approx\ell_{\rm P}$ the cliff sits at 320 (ion zone) to 333 (superconducting die) qubits, not 232–245 (8.7). Independently, if the $O(k^4a^2)$ term in Eq. Feasibility of S16 is better than the manuscript's cautious tone suggests. ### 3.5 The knot, $\hbar$ and spin

Lemma 4 is kinematics ($E_{\rm rot}/E = j/2$ follows from $L = j\hbar$ and $E = \hbar\omega$ for any rigid rotor); the manuscript says so and should not count it as content. Theorem 8 and Proposition 4 are correct and are the real content: the solvability window $j < 2a/(a+b)$ depends on how the field energy and the rotational energy scale under dilation. The commitment to $(1,1)$ (Flag F-B1) is flagged, which is honest, but its physical meaning deserves a sentence the manuscript omits. $b$ is the dilation exponent of the moment of inertia; for a texture whose inertia is carried by the quadratic gradient term, $\mathbb I\propto V^3$ and $b = 3$, which gives $j < 1/2$ and *excludes* $j = \tfrac12$ (5.6). $(1,1)$ corresponds to inertia carried by the quartic term. So F-B1 is not an innocent constitutive choice; it is the statement that the electron's spin inertia is Skyrme-term dominated, and the bench (S13) can measure $b$ directly through the dilation law $V(j)$ (Section 4.10).

The two closure conditions with the same $\hbar$ are the heart of the "closure of Planck's constant", and here the revision's re-grading ("constrained, not derived") is right: $\hbar = \mathfrak c\Lambda\sqrt J$ reduces three unknowns to two. It is one relation, not a derivation, and the mass–inertia–$\hbar$ triangle still has a free side.

### 3.8 Cosmology

The relaxation family (Propositions 11–14) is internally impeccable; every derivation was followed and every number reproduces. The question is empirical, and it is going badly. Against the DESI DR2 CPL posteriors, with a Gaussian proxy and an assumed correlation $\rho(w_0,w_a) = -0.9$ (the proxy reproduces DESI's own quoted 2.8σ and 4.2σ for $\Lambda$CDM to within 0.3σ), the family's members are at $\Delta\chi^2 = 9$–$19$ for $n=1$, $13$–$19$ for $n = 0.9$, $35$–$39$ for $n = 0.75$ and $140$–$169$ for the mechanistically selected $n = 1/2$ (8.4). The reason is directional (8.3): in the family $w$ rises toward zero in the past ($w(z=1) = -0.61$ at $n=\tfrac12$), whereas the data prefer $w$ below $-1$ in the past and above $-1$ today. The family's best point is its empty member, $\Lambda$CDM.

The pre-registered retirement condition is reasonable ("a parametrization that does not force the crossing"), and it is true that part of the DESI preference comes from the supernova compilations and the BAO–CMB tension at $z\sim0.5$–$1$. But GUM should not wait for Euclid to do the obvious: fit the family itself, with its single parameter $n$ and $\Sigma m_\nu \ge 0.058$ eV, to DESI DR2 BAO + Planck + each supernova set, and report the posterior on $n$. The manuscript lists this as S18 "executed"; no fit is shown. Section 4.5 asks for it.

The coupling that nobody has drawn: Proposition 20 makes the observed birefringence $\beta \ne 0$ *require* $n < 1$ (static $\Theta$ rotates nothing), while DESI pushes $n\to1$. If LiteBIRD confirms $\beta\ne0$ at 5σ and Euclid confirms $w_a<0$, GUM's dark-energy sector and its electromagnetic chirality sector cannot both survive; if $\beta\to0$ and $w\to-1$, GUM survives only in the corner where it predicts nothing about either. The manuscript treats S19 and S20 as independent stakes. They are one stake with two readouts. And Proposition 20 has a second consequence the manuscript misses: because $\rho_{\rm DE}(t)$ was enormously larger at recombination for any $n<1$, the rotation accrues almost entirely early. For a polarized source at redshift $z$, $\beta(z)/\beta_{\rm CMB} \propto [H(z)^{2(1-n)}-1]/[H(z_{\rm rec})^{2(1-n)}-1]$, which is $4\times10^{-5}$ at $z=1$ and $5\times10^{-4}$ at $z=7$ for $n=\tfrac12$, and still only $10^{-1}$ at $z=7$ for $n=0.9$ (8.5). That is a clean discriminator against axion-like explanations (Section 4.4).

Gravity (Proposition 29) is Sakharov induction plus the Kondo–Bilby–Kröner–Kleinert dictionary. The positivity claim is fine. The closure K-16 ($c_{\rm GW} = c$ "identically at tree level") follows from a *stipulation*, "when the edge comparator is the sole carrier of restoring energy and inertia", which fixes $J = \tfrac12\rho_0\ell_g^2$ and $\gamma = 4\mu\ell_g^2$. That is an assumption presented as a closure; the revision keeps K-16 posed, correctly. The quantitative content the manuscript misses is the one drawn in Section 3.4: Eq. (75) fixes $a$.

### 3.9 Dark matter

Proposition 30's sterile $\tau$-mass knot is the most interesting new object in the manuscript and the least examined. Two things follow from its own statements. First, with $g_{hNN} = m_\tau/v = 7.2\times10^{-3}$ and $2M_{N} < M_h$, $\Gamma(h\to N_0\bar N_0) = \Gamma(h\to\tau^+\tau^-) = 0.26$ MeV, so ${\rm BR}(h\to{\rm inv}) = 5.9\%$ for a Dirac knot, 3.1% for Majorana (8.9). The current ATLAS limit is 10.7%; HL-LHC projections reach ≈2.5%. This is a real, near-term, discriminating prediction, and it is not in the stakes table. Second, if that pair channel is open the relic density is not freeze-in but freeze-out, and a 1.78 GeV Higgs-portal fermion with annihilation $\langle\sigma v\rangle\sim3\times10^{-36}$ cm³/s is overabundant by $10^{10}$ (8.11). The manuscript's way out, $\mathcal F_{\rm pinch} = 2\times10^{-8}$ on knot-number-changing processes, closes the relic problem and the invisible-width prediction together, but it then owes an explanation of why $h\to\tau^+\tau^-$ and $e^+e^-$ pair creation, which also create degree $\pm1$ knot pairs, proceed at full rate. Lemma 5 does not help: pair creation conserves total degree and needs no $\det\mathsf F\to0$ pinch. Either the sterile knot is produced at Yukawa strength (then it is a thermal relic and K-17 as written is dead, and the Higgs invisible width is the test) or there is a selection rule distinguishing dressed from undressed pairs, which would be new physics the manuscript should state. Direct detection cannot help: $\sigma_{\rm SI}\approx10^{-47}$ cm² at 1.8 GeV sits under the solar-neutrino fog (8.10).

## 4. Extrapolation: new questions, with GUM's answers derived and the experiments that adjudicate

Each item states a question the manuscript does not ask, what GUM is committed to answering (derived from its own equations), the experiment, and what each outcome means. Numbers are from `verification-report.md` rows E.1–E.7 and 8.x.

### 4.1 Does the Coulomb field of a kicked charge update before the light cone?

*GUM (manuscript).* Theorem 5(ii): the longitudinal field at distance $r$ updates after $r/c_L$, with $c_L > 10^4c$, so the Coulomb part of the field of a charge whose motion changes at $t=0$ should appear at remote sensors essentially instantaneously, with the full Coulomb amplitude; only the *radiated* longitudinal power is suppressed by $(c/c_L)^3$. *GUM (revision):* no local completion has a precursor; the near-field experiment is a null test.
*Experiment.* A 1 nC relativistic bunch deflected by 1 mm in 1 ns; a ring of electro-optic or antenna sensors at 10 m, gated. The Coulomb update is $\Delta E\approx9\times10^{-6}$ V/m arriving within 3 ps; the radiation pulse is $3\times10^{-3}$ V/m at 33 ns (E.1). The two are separated by 33 ns, so the measurement is a timing measurement, not an amplitude one, and averaging over $10^6$ bunches makes a μV/m precursor accessible. De Sangro et al. (2015) looked at the related question of a beam exiting a pipe; a *kicked* bunch isolates the acceleration event.
*Outcomes.* A precursor at the Coulomb amplitude is the discovery of a second speed, and a violation of laboratory-frame causality that only a preferred frame can absorb. A null at the μV/m level retires the manuscript's Theorem 5(ii) and confirms the revision's locality dichotomy. Either way one of the two drafts is wrong, which makes this the cheapest experiment with a guaranteed GUM-relevant answer.

### 4.2 What does finite $c_L$ do in three-party configurations?

*GUM.* The tower updates at $c_L$ in the substrate frame. By Bancal et al. (2012) and Barnea et al. (2013), a finite-speed influence model that reproduces quantum correlations for $v$-connected pairs either signals superluminally or deviates from quantum mechanics in configurations where some pairs are $v$-connected and others are not. GUM must choose. Proposition 26's error term $r_{SM}/(c_L\Delta t)$ already says that sequential measurements faster than $r/c_L$ deviate.
*Experiment.* Three parties on a 10-km fibre triangle with 100-ps synchronisation; the CMB dipole is the natural candidate for the substrate frame, so the configuration is swept sidereally. With $c_L > 10^4c$, "not $v$-connected" requires events within 3 ns per km of separation, which fibre-based entanglement sources and superconducting-nanowire detectors already achieve. The observable is the Barnea inequality for the tripartite state (an $(N,v)$-causal bound); a violation while timing excludes influence between a chosen pair is a deviation-or-signalling verdict.
*Outcomes.* If GUM deviates from quantum mechanics, it must deviate here first; a null to $10^{-3}$ in the tripartite correlator pushes $c_L$ beyond $10^5c$ and, through Eq. (14), $P_L/P_\perp$ below $10^{-15}$. If GUM instead holds that the tower's hidden influences never fail to arrive (infinite $c_L$ in effect), it has given up the second speed and S15 with it.

### 4.3 Does the fidelity cliff move when the register is spread out?

*GUM.* $n_{\max}\approx\log_2(V/a^3)$ counts substrate cells on the register's support. The same circuit on the same qubits, with the register dispersed over a larger volume, should cliff later: +23 qubits for $10^{-10}\to10^{-3}$ m³, +33 for a 1 m³ photonically networked register (4.8). The revision's S29 makes the same point with three hosting hypotheses (volume, cut area, qubit extent) that give different slopes.
*Experiment.* Mirror-peaked circuits at fixed depth on (i) a compact ion crystal, (ii) the same ions split between two traps linked by photonic interconnects, (iii) a neutral-atom array at two lattice spacings. The protocol of Section 4.4 of the manuscript applies; the signature is a depth-independent step whose location shifts with geometry while noise-model errors do not.
*Outcomes.* A geometry-dependent cliff is unexplainable by any noise model and would be the discovery of a spatial bookkeeping substrate. A flat return probability through $n\approx350$ on a compact register retires dense storage for $a\ge\ell_{\rm P}$ (the gravity-fixed value), leaving only Schmidt hosting or sub-Planckian structure. Note that the corrected cliff width (2.8 qubits, Section 2.3) makes the step easier to resolve than the manuscript's 6.

### 4.4 Is the cosmic birefringence a recombination-era effect?

*GUM.* From Proposition 20 and Eq. (63), $\beta(z)\propto\rho_{\rm DE}(z)-\rho_{\rm DE}(0)$. For every $n<1$ this is dominated by the early universe: $\beta(z=7)/\beta_{\rm CMB} = 5\times10^{-4}$ ($n=\tfrac12$), $1.7\times10^{-2}$ ($n=0.75$), $0.10$ ($n=0.9$) (8.5). The low-$\ell$ reionization bump in the CMB EB spectrum should therefore show at most a tenth of the rotation of the high-$\ell$ recombination signal, and polarized radio galaxies and quasars at $z\lesssim5$ should show almost none ($\lesssim0.02^\circ$ even at $n=0.9$, $\lesssim0.002^\circ$ for $n\le0.75$).
*Experiment.* LiteBIRD's low-$\ell$ EB versus its high-$\ell$ EB (the former is its design target); the existing Planck/ACT high-$\ell$ value as the denominator; a stacked rotation-measure-corrected polarization-angle survey of $z\sim1$–$4$ radio galaxies (SKA pathfinders) as the low-redshift arm.
*Outcomes.* Equal low-$\ell$ and high-$\ell$ rotation (the generic axion prediction for $m_a\gg H(z_{\rm reion})$) retires Proposition 20. A low-$\ell$ deficit of the predicted size would be strong evidence for a dark-energy-driven rotation, and its magnitude would *measure* $n$ independently of DESI: $\beta_{\rm reion}/\beta_{\rm rec}$ is a monotone function of $n$ (8.5).

### 4.5 What value of $n$ does the data actually allow, with the neutrino mass included?

*GUM.* One parameter, $n\in[\tfrac12,1]$, plus $\Sigma m_\nu\ge0.058$ eV. The CPL proxy says $n=1$ is the least bad member and $n=\tfrac12$ is excluded at $\gtrsim11$σ-equivalent (8.4), but a proxy is not a fit, and the family's $w(a)$ is not linear in $a$.
*Experiment (computational).* Fit $H(a)$ from Eq. (49) directly to DESI DR2 BAO distances, the Planck distance priors, and each of Pantheon+, Union3, DESY5, with $n$ and $\Sigma m_\nu$ free and the neutrino prior from S1. Report the posterior on $n$ and the Bayes factor against $\Lambda$CDM. This is what S18 "executed" should have meant.
*Outcomes.* A posterior confined to $n>0.95$ leaves Proposition 19's diffusive selection ($n=\tfrac12$) dead and the radiative channel barely alive, and it feeds Section 4.4: $\beta_{\rm reion}/\beta_{\rm rec}$ at $n=0.95$ is $\approx0.2$, so LiteBIRD becomes the decisive instrument. A posterior excluding $n>0.95$ as well retires the sector now, before Euclid.

### 4.6 What is the Higgs invisible width, and are sterile knot pairs made at Yukawa strength?

*GUM.* ${\rm BR}(h\to N_0\bar N_0) = 5.9\%$ (Dirac) or $3.1\%$ (Majorana) if knot-pair creation is unsuppressed (8.9); $\approx0$ if the pinch suppression applies to all knot-number-changing processes. The same question decides whether K-17's relic density is freeze-in (as printed) or a $10^{10}$-fold freeze-out overabundance (8.11).
*Experiment.* HL-LHC $h\to$ invisible in VBF and $Zh$; the projected 95% reach of ≈2.5% covers both the Dirac and the Majorana case. In parallel, the manuscript must state the selection rule: what distinguishes $e^+e^-$ pair creation (degree $\pm1$, dressed) from $N_0\bar N_0$ (degree $\pm1$, undressed)? Lemma 5 gives none.
*Outcomes.* ${\rm BR}_{\rm inv}$ at the 3–6% level with no other new physics would be a spectacular confirmation of a τ-mass sterile knot and would kill K-17's freeze-in story at the same time (the knot would be a thermal relic, overabundant, so it could not be the dark matter). ${\rm BR}_{\rm inv}<2.5\%$ forces the selection rule and makes the sterile knot undetectable except gravitationally. Either way the dark-matter closure as printed does not survive.

### 4.7 Does the lepton mass spectrum drift with the dark energy?

*GUM.* The electron mass depends on the pitch through $A(q) = A_{\rm halo}+\kappa_{\rm far}\ln(1/q\lambda_{\rm halo})$ and $\Sigma(2) = 2A+B$: $d\ln m_e/d\ln q = \kappa_{\rm far} = 0.1065$, $d\ln m_\mu/d\ln q = 0.053$ (E.4). The dark-energy mechanism is a lag $\delta q(t)\propto H^{1-n}$ (Appendix D). Hence $m_e/m_\tau$ drifts cosmologically, and $\mu = m_p/m_e$ with it unless the quark sector drifts identically.
*Constraint.* Quasar molecular-hydrogen bounds, $|\delta\mu/\mu|<10^{-6}$ at $z\approx2$–$4$, give $|\delta q/q^*(z=3)-\delta q/q^*(0)|<9\times10^{-6}$, i.e. $(\delta q/q^*)_0<8\times10^{-6}$ for $n=\tfrac12$ (E.4). Through Eq. (44), $\rho_{\rm DE}\simeq\tfrac12\chi^{-1}\delta q^2$, that is a lower bound on the soft-mode stiffness: $\chi^{-1}\gtrsim 2\rho_{{\rm DE},0}/(8\times10^{-6}q^*)^2\approx4\times10^{2}$ eV². A pitch-scale stiffness $\chi^{-1}\sim q^{*2}$ would give $\delta\mu/\mu\approx4\times10^{-4}$ at $z=3$, excluded 400-fold; a GeV-scale stiffness gives $10^{-14}$, safe (E.5). The manuscript never states $\chi^{-1}$.
*Experiment.* ESPRESSO/ELT $\mu$-variation at $z\sim2$–$4$ at the $10^{-7}$ level; optical-clock drift bounds locally ($10^{-18}$/yr, which GUM passes easily since the present-day lag changes on a Hubble time). *Outcomes.* The manuscript owes one number, $\chi^{-1}$, and with it either a prediction for $\delta\mu/\mu(z)$ or a demonstration that it is below $10^{-9}$. "Neutrinos lighter in the past" (the revision's Sec. IX E) is the same drift seen from the other end, and the two must be quoted together: $\delta\ln m_3 = \delta\ln q$ while $\delta\ln m_e = 0.1065\,\delta\ln q$.

### 4.8 Is the vacuum pitch spatially uniform at the 26-μm scale?

*GUM.* The condensate is an amorphous double-twist network ("blue fog") with cells of order $p\approx26$ μm separated by disclination lines. The pitch $q^* = \chi_s/\gamma_s$ is a material constant and does not vary cell to cell; the helix *orientation* and the proximity to network disclinations do. If any part of $A$ or $B$ depends on the local environment, $m_e$ varies on the 26-μm scale.
*Constraints already in hand.* Optical lattice clocks compare atoms at sites micrometres apart at $10^{-18}$; electron interferometers with 100-μm path separations hold $10^{-2}$ rad over nanosecond flights, which bounds cell-to-cell $\delta m_e/m_e<10^{-14}$ (E.3). So the frustration integrals must be environment-blind to at least 14 and plausibly 18 decades.
*Experiment.* A deliberately *dedicated* measurement: an optical lattice clock with the lattice scanned over a 100-μm range in 1-μm steps, looking for a 26-μm-periodic or cell-structured modulation of the transition frequency at $10^{-18}$. This has not been done as a search; it is a reanalysis of existing clock-comparison data plus a short dedicated run.
*Outcomes.* A null is expected by GUM and should be *claimed* by GUM as evidence for halo dominance (this is the honest replacement for Proposition 18's broken proof: the belts must be in the halo because otherwise the fog's cell structure would print on $m_e$). A positive signal with 26-μm structure would be beyond anything in this manuscript.

### 4.9 Can the frustration integrals be made cell-blind without K-10, and what replaces Proposition 18?

*GUM.* The halo is a linear, gapped, spherically symmetric evanescent field; its integrals against a uniform-pitch helix depend on $q\lambda_{\rm halo}$ only. The core is a BPS compacton, rigid to $O(\epsilon)$. A frustration belt that tried to sit in the core would deform a BPS profile at cost $O(\Lambda\tilde m)$ per unit deformation, while sitting in the halo costs $O(a_\psi/\lambda_{\rm halo})$. The correct variational statement is a *comparison of two scales*, $\Lambda\tilde m$ versus $a_\psi/\lambda_{\rm halo}$, not the shell integrals of Eq. (57).
*Deliverable.* Re-derive K-10 as: (i) the core's rigidity theorem (the BPS profile has no zero modes other than $SDiff$, so a belt cannot be absorbed at $O(\epsilon^0)$), (ii) the halo's linear response (a belt of class $p$ is a solution of the gapped linear equation with winding boundary condition, whose energy is a computable function of $q\lambda_{\rm halo}$), (iii) the ratio. This is a few pages of linear analysis, and it would also yield $A_{\rm halo}$ and $\kappa_{\rm far}$ in closed form, converting two [N] numbers into [DF] ones and settling Section 2.4's fit-versus-prediction question.

### 4.12 Is there a longitudinal radiation channel at all?

*GUM (manuscript).* $P_L/P_\perp = \eta(c/c_L)^3\lesssim10^{-12}$, radiated by $\partial_t\rho_q$, hence with the angular pattern of a longitudinal (compressional) wave: maximal *along* the dipole axis, where transverse radiation vanishes identically.
*Experiment.* A high-power narrow-band dipole emitter and an on-axis, line-of-sight-polarized resonant detector (a capacitive sensor driven only by a field component along the propagation direction), with the transverse null as the zero-background geometry. The long longitudinal wavelength ($\lambda_L = c_L/\omega\ge10^4\lambda$) means the field is quasi-uniform over the detector, which simplifies the sensor but needs a geometry that rejects the near-field of the emitter; a 100-wavelength baseline does it.
*Outcomes.* Any on-axis longitudinal signal is the substrate. A null is a direct, frame-independent bound on $c_L$ that does not depend on identifying the preferred frame (unlike Bell timing).
